Check-in notes
Local tip: Tokyo hotels rarely allow check-in before 15:00, but nearly all hold luggage for free from the morning — drop your bags and start exploring. Airport limousine buses and “takkyubin” luggage forwarding (about ¥2,000–2,600 per bag) can also send suitcases ahead to your next city.

02 — Before You Fly

Pre-Trip Checklist 出発前の準備

8–4 Weeks Out
  • Passport valid for entire stay
  • Book flights & first hotels
  • Check visa / visa-waiver rules for your country
  • Reserve big-ticket sights (Ghibli Museum, teamLab, Shibuya Sky sell out weeks ahead)
  • Compare a JR Pass vs. single tickets — it only pays off with 2+ long shinkansen legs
2–1 Weeks Out
  • Fill in Visit Japan Web (immigration & customs QR)
  • Buy an eSIM or pocket Wi-Fi
  • Travel insurance
  • Notify your bank / enable overseas card use
Money & Apps
  • Add Suica/PASMO to Apple Pay or plan a Welcome Suica card
  • Plan cash: Japan is card-friendly now, but small restaurants, shrines and markets still want yen
  • Download: Google Maps, Google Translate (offline JP), Japan Travel by Navitime
  • Note your country’s embassy address
Local tip: 7-Eleven ATMs accept nearly every foreign card, are everywhere, and have English menus — there is no need to exchange large amounts at the airport. Convenience stores are also where you’ll buy transit-friendly breakfasts for ¥300–500.

05 — Local Knowledge

Tokyo: A First-Timer’s 3 Days 東京3日間モデルコース

A realistic pace — two neighborhoods per day, not seven. Written and walk-tested by a Tokyo local based in Shinjuku.

Day 1West Tokyo — Shinjuku & Shibuya
AMShinjuku Gyoen at 09:00 opening (¥500, calm before crowds) → walk to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building free observatory (202 m, no ticket needed)
PMMeiji Shrine & Harajuku → Shibuya: the Scramble from street level first, then Hachiko. Book Shibuya Sky for sunset weeks ahead — or skip it and keep your yen
NightBack to Shinjuku: Omoide Yokocho’s lantern alleys for yakitori, then a stroll through neon Kabukicho (look, don’t follow touts)
Day 2East Tokyo — Asakusa, Ueno & Akihabara
AMSenso-ji before 09:00 (the difference in crowds is night and day) → Nakamise snacks → Sumida River terrace views of Tokyo Skytree
PMUeno Park & one museum (Tokyo National Museum if you pick just one) → Ameyoko market street → Akihabara arcades and retro-game floors
NightIzakaya dinner under the Yurakucho railway arches — order by pointing, everyone does
Day 3Bay Side — Tsukiji, Ginza & teamLab
AMTsukiji Outer Market breakfast (go hungry: tamagoyaki, tuna bowls, oysters) → Hamarikyu Gardens tea house
PMGinza — depachika food halls in the basements are free to wander and unforgettable → teamLab Planets (buy tickets online, timed entry)
NightTokyo Station’s Ramen Street, then the Marunouchi red-brick station facade lit up — a quiet, perfect last photo

06 — Local Knowledge

Day Trips from Tokyo 東京発・日帰り旅

All reachable from Shinjuku or Tokyo Station without a JR Pass. Times are one-way.

WhereFrom / TimeApprox. CostWhy Go / Local Notes
HakoneShinjuku · 85 min¥6,100 Free-passOnsen, ropeway over volcanic steam, Lake Ashi. The Hakone Freepass covers the whole loop — do it counter-clockwise to beat tour groups
KamakuraTokyo Stn · 60 min≈ ¥1,900 returnGreat Buddha, hydrangea temples, Enoden seaside tram. Go on a weekday; weekends are packed by 11:00
NikkoAsakusa · 110 min≈ ¥5,700 passToshogu Shrine’s carved gates and cedar forests. Autumn color peaks late Oct–early Nov
KawagoeShinjuku · 55 min≈ ¥1,000 return“Little Edo” clay-walled merchant street, sweet-potato everything, kimono rental town
Mt. TakaoShinjuku · 50 min≈ ¥800 returnTokyo’s easiest mountain — cable car, monkey park, Fuji views on clear winter mornings
YokohamaShibuya · 30 min≈ ¥600 returnRamen Museum, Chinatown, harbor night views from the Cosmo Clock ferris wheel
Local tip: For Hakone and Kawagoe, buy the private-railway “free pass” at Shinjuku’s Odakyu or Seibu counters — they bundle the round trip with unlimited local buses and small discounts, and staff speak English. Check typhoon forecasts in September; mountain ropeways stop in high wind.

08 — Eat

Japan Food Bucket List 食べたいものリスト

The Classics
  • Counter sushi (lunch sets are the smart splurge)
  • Ramen — try two styles: shoyu & tonkotsu
  • Tempura over rice (tendon)
  • Wagyu — yakiniku or a steak lunch
  • Izakaya night: yakitori + a lemon sour
  • Okonomiyaki or monjayaki (Tokyo’s own)
  • Proper matcha & wagashi in a garden tea house
Cheap & Unmissable
  • Conbini breakfast: onigiri + egg sando
  • Standing soba at a train station (¥400)
  • Depachika food hall grazing
  • Taiyaki or melonpan, eaten warm
  • Vending-machine hot corn soup (winter)
  • Strawberry daifuku from a street stall
  • A ¥120 seasonal KitKat you can’t get home
Local tip: Lines in Japan usually mean “worth it,” but for ramen a 15-minute queue at 11:20 becomes 45 minutes by noon — eat lunch early (11:00–11:30) or late (14:00) and you will almost never wait.

09 — Pack

Packing List 持ち物リスト

Documents & Money
  • Passport (+ photo of it in your phone)
  • Visit Japan Web QR screenshot
  • Two credit/debit cards, kept separately
  • Coin purse — you will collect coins fast
Tech
  • eSIM installed / pocket Wi-Fi booked
  • Power bank (long train days)
  • Plug type A adapter (100V, same as US shape)
Japan-Specific Smart Packs
  • Slip-on shoes — you’ll remove them at temples, some restaurants & fitting rooms
  • Small hand towel (many restrooms have no dryers)
  • Foldable bag for trash — public bins are rare, you carry it until a conbini
  • Comfortable socks without holes (see: shoe removal)
  • Half-empty suitcase — you will fill it
  • Layers: Tokyo swings 10°C+ between morning and afternoon in spring/autumn
