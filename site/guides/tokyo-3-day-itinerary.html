Tokyo in 3 Days: A First-Timer’s Itinerary That Isn’t a Death March

Updated September 2026 · walk-tested from Shinjuku

Two neighborhoods a day. Real prices in yen. The opening-hour tricks that make the same places feel twice as good.

Day 1 — West Tokyo: Shinjuku & Shibuya

Morning. Start at Shinjuku Gyoen when the gates open at 9:00 (¥500). You get an hour of near-empty gardens before the tour buses arrive — locals know the difference between Gyoen at 9 and Gyoen at 11 is the difference between Kyoto calm and Disneyland. Walk 15 minutes to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building: the 202-meter observatory is completely free, no ticket, and on clear days you’ll see Mt. Fuji.

Afternoon. Train two stops to Harajuku for Meiji Shrine’s forest, then walk down to Shibuya. See the Scramble from street level first — it’s better inside the crowd than above it. If you want the rooftop view, Shibuya Sky sells out days to weeks ahead; book online or spend the ¥2,500 on dinner instead.

Evening. Come back to Shinjuku for Omoide Yokocho: lantern-lit alleys of eight-seat yakitori counters. Sit where there’s space, point at what your neighbor’s having, and you’ll be fine. Finish with a walk through Kabukicho’s neon — look freely, but skip anyone inviting you into a bar.

Local tip: Hotels hold your bags free from the morning even when check-in is 15:00. Drop luggage first — Day 1 is much better without a suitcase.

Day 2 — East Tokyo: Asakusa, Ueno & Akihabara

Morning. Be at Senso-ji before 9:00. At 8am the incense drifts over a nearly empty courtyard; by 11 it’s shoulder to shoulder. Snack your way down Nakamise street, then walk the Sumida River terrace for the best free view of Tokyo Skytree.

Afternoon. Ueno Park — if you only enter one museum in Japan, make it the Tokyo National Museum (¥1,000). Then wander Ameyoko’s market chaos, and end in Akihabara for retro-game floors and arcades.

Evening. Dinner under the railway arches at Yurakucho: smoky, loud, cheap, unforgettable.

Day 3 — The bay: Tsukiji, Ginza & teamLab

Morning. Arrive at Tsukiji Outer Market hungry. Tamagoyaki on a stick, a tuna bowl, grilled scallops — budget ¥2,000–3,000 and graze. Walk it off in Hamarikyu Gardens and have matcha in the tea house on the pond.

Afternoon. Ginza’s department-store basement food halls (depachika) are free theater. Then teamLab Planets — buy the timed ticket online days ahead.

Evening. Ramen Street inside Tokyo Station, then step outside to the lit-up red-brick Marunouchi facade for a quiet last photo.

What this trip costs

  • Budget style: ¥6,000–9,000/day — conbini breakfasts, one sit-down meal, free sights
  • Mid-range: ¥12,000–20,000/day — restaurants, paid museums and views
  • Transit: ¥800–1,200/day on an IC card (Suica in Apple Pay works out of the box)
